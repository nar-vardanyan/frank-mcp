# Customer Interview Questions Template

**In short:** Use this list as a starting interview guide, not as a script you must read word for word. Ask about a recent event, keep the questions open, and follow up when an answer is short or unclear. ## How to use this template

1. Write one sentence for the decision this interview should change.
2. Copy the core list below, then keep 6 to 8 questions. Add a follow-up under each one for when the first answer is thin.
3. Replace [job], [product], and [event] with the customer's words, not your internal names.
4. Take the interview yourself before you share it, and cut leading or hypothetical questions.
5. Keep a consistent core across participants so you can compare answers.

Ask "Tell me about the last time you…" rather than "Would you use…?" or "How much would you pay…?" Open questions let people answer in their own words ([Nielsen Norman Group, Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/open-ended-questions/)). The critical incident technique asks people to recall a specific time when something affected an outcome, rather than to give general opinions ([The Critical Incident Technique in UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/critical-incident-technique/)).

For the full method, see [How to run customer interviews](how-to-run-customer-interviews.md).

## Core questions (copy these)

Use this list when you want a general customer interview about a job someone already does.

1. Tell me about the last time you tried to [job]. What were you trying to get done?
2. What did you try first? What did you try after that?
3. Where did you get stuck, or what took longer than you expected?
4. Who else was involved, and what did they care about?
5. What do you use now to get this done? What do you like about it, and what do you work around?
6. Walk me through what happened the last time this went badly.
7. What almost made you give up?
8. If you could change only one thing about how you do this today, what would it be?
9. What did you expect that did not happen?
10. Is there anything I should have asked, or anything you thought about that we did not cover?

Follow-ups that work with almost every question: "Can you walk me through that moment?" "What happened next?" "What did you do then?" "Who else did you talk to?" Avoid "why did you do that?" as a first follow-up if it makes people defend themselves; ask what happened instead.

## Short variants

Keep the same rules. Swap the core list for one of these when the decision is narrower.

**Churn or cancellation**

1. Walk me through the moment you decided to cancel.
2. What had changed, compared with when you signed up?
3. What almost made you stay?
4. What do you use now, including a spreadsheet or "nothing"?
5. What, if anything, would have changed your mind, and when would it have needed to happen?

A longer method: [How to find out why customers churn](how-to-find-out-why-customers-churn.md).

**Startup idea or discovery**

1. Tell me about the last time you tried to [job].
2. What did you try first, and where did you get stuck?
3. What do you use now, and what do you pay for it (in money or time)?
4. When was the last time you actively looked for a better way?
5. If this problem disappeared tomorrow, what would change in your week?

A longer method: [How to validate a startup idea with customer interviews](validate-a-startup-idea-with-customer-interviews.md).

**Product-market fit**

1. How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]? Very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, or not disappointed?
2. What made you choose that answer?
3. What is the main benefit you get from [product]?
4. Tell me about the last time it really helped you.
5. What would you use instead, and what is missing today?

A longer method: [How to measure product-market fit with customer interviews](measure-product-market-fit-with-customer-interviews.md).

**Win-loss (a recent deal)**

1. Walk me through how you decided.
2. Why did you choose us, the other option, or nothing?
3. What almost tipped it the other way?
4. Who else was involved, and what did they care about?
5. What would have needed to be true for the decision to go the other way?

## FAQ

### Can I use this template without Frank?

Yes. It is a question list. Use it on a live call, in a document, or in any interview tool.

### How many questions should I ask?

Enough to cover the decision, few enough that people finish. Six to eight open questions, each with a follow-up, fits in about 10 minutes for many studies. Match the length to your plan if you use Frank: the Free plan allows interviews up to 10 minutes, and paid plans allow 20 or 30.

### Should I read the questions word for word?

Use them as a guide. Keep the core wording consistent enough to compare answers, and follow an interesting detail even if it is not the next line on the list.

### Does Frank find people to interview?

No. Frank does not recruit participants. You invite people from your own product or email list.
