## Sample 8-question guide

Use this guide when you need to understand buyer trust, a security review, or alert fatigue. Keep all eight questions if they serve that decision, or cut the ones that do not. Replace [product] with the name buyers or users use, and add a follow-up under any question where the first answer is thin. Invite buyers or users from deals or accounts you already have. Frank does not recruit participants.

1. Walk me through the last time your team evaluated a tool like [product]. What prompted the search?
2. What did the security review look like, and what almost blocked the purchase?
3. Who else was involved, and what did each person need to see before they would trust it?
4. Tell me about a recent week of alerts. Which ones did you act on, and which did you ignore?
5. When an alert is noisy or unclear, what do you do, and what does that cost you?
6. What would make you switch away from a tool in this category, including after a missed finding or a failed review?
7. After you bought, what took longer than you expected before the team relied on it day to day?
8. If you were choosing again tomorrow, what would you need to see earlier?

## FAQ

### What should I ask in a cybersecurity customer interview?

Ask about a recent evaluation or a week of alerts: what the security review looked like, who else had to trust the tool, which alerts people act on or ignore, and what would make them switch. Use the eight questions on this page as a starting guide.

### Does Frank recruit cybersecurity buyers?

No. Frank AI Researcher does not recruit participants. You invite buyers or users from deals or accounts you already have.

### Why interview about trust, security review, and alerts?

A ticket or a missed alert can show that something failed. An interview can show how the security review went, who had to sign off, and which alerts people ignore. Use the guide when that story would change a product or go-to-market decision.
