# How to Find Out Why Customers Churn

**In short:** To find out why customers churn, talk to people who cancelled recently, ideally within days, and ask them to walk through what happened: the moment they started doubting the product, what they tried, and what they switched to. A cancellation survey tells you the category; a short voice interview tells you the story behind it.

## Why cancellation surveys are not enough

Most cancel flows ask one multiple-choice question: too expensive, missing a feature, not using it enough. Those answers are a useful count, but they are closed-ended by design. Closed questions are good for measuring how often something happens; open-ended questions are what surface reasons you did not think to list ([Nielsen Norman Group, Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/open-ended-questions/)).

"Too expensive" might mean the price went up, the team shrank, the value was never clear, or a cheaper tool did "good enough". Each of those calls for a different fix. You only learn which one by asking a follow-up, which a dropdown can't do.

## What to look for in a churn conversation

Churn usually has a moment, not just a reason. The critical incident technique, a long-established research method, asks people to recall a specific time when something affected an outcome, rather than to give general opinions ([Nielsen Norman Group, The Critical Incident Technique in UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/critical-incident-technique/)). For churn, the outcome is cancelling, so you ask about the specific events that led there.

Listen for four things:

- **The trigger.** What changed: a new manager, a budget review, a failed rollout, a competitor demo.
- **The struggle.** What they were trying to do with your product that didn't work, in their words.
- **The alternative.** What they use now, including a spreadsheet or "nothing".
- **The save point.** What, if anything, would have kept them, and when it would have needed to happen.

## Step by step

### 1. Decide what you will change

Write one sentence: we will interview [recently cancelled customers in this segment] to decide [what], for example which onboarding step to rebuild or whether the entry plan is priced wrong. Churn research without a decision turns into a list of complaints.

### 2. Write 6 to 8 questions about what happened

Ask about recent, specific events. A starting set:

1. Take me back to when you first signed up. What were you hoping it would do?
2. When did you first feel it might not be working out? What happened?
3. What did you try before deciding to cancel?
4. Who else was involved in the decision?
5. What are you using instead now, if anything?
6. If you could change one thing about the product, what would it be?
7. Is there anything that would have changed your mind? When?

Avoid "Why did you leave?" as the first question; it invites a short, polite answer. Avoid defending the product or pitching a fix during the interview. More examples: [churn interviews](https://www.hifrank.ai/use-case/ai-churn-interviews) and [how to ask better customer interview questions](https://www.hifrank.ai/blog/how-to-ask-better-customer-interview-questions).

### 3. Ask soon after cancellation

Memory fades and people move on. Put the invite where the decision happens: a link on the cancellation confirmation page, in the confirmation email, or a follow-up a few days later. Keep it short and say why you are asking. Many teams offer a small thank-you, but make it clear the interview is not a sales call.

You need people who actually cancelled. Frank does not recruit participants, so the invite has to come from your own product or email list.

### 4. Remove the scheduling step

Cancelled customers rarely book a 30-minute call. AI-moderated interviews let people answer when it suits them, without a human facilitator ([Nielsen Norman Group, AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). A short voice interview from a link in the cancellation email asks much less of them than a calendar invite. Keep live calls for large accounts, where a relationship conversation is worth your time.

### 5. Read transcripts, then group by trigger

Read full transcripts, not only summaries. Group stories by trigger and by segment (plan, company size, tenure). Check that each theme you act on is backed by exact quotes in context. Stop when new interviews stop adding new triggers; for a fairly similar group, themes often settle within about a dozen conversations ([Guest, Bunce & Johnson, 2006](https://www.sfu.ca/~palys/GuestEtAl-2006-HowManyInterviewsAreEnough.pdf)). Then use your cancellation survey or billing data to size the themes you found.

### 6. Close the loop

Write a one-page readout: top triggers, what you will change, what you will not, and who owns it. Re-run the same interview a month or two after a fix ships to see if the stories change.

## When this is a poor fit

If very few customers cancel, call them yourself. If the churn question needs deep domain knowledge or a look at the customer's screen, a human interviewer is better; Nielsen Norman Group found AI interviewers work best as a complement to human moderation ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). And if you need to know how many customers left for each reason, use your survey and billing data, not interview counts.

## FAQ

### What is the best way to find out why customers churn?
Talk to people who recently cancelled and ask them to walk through what happened, from signing up to deciding to leave. Use your cancellation survey to count reasons, and interviews to understand them.

### When should I interview churned customers?
As soon after cancellation as you can, while they remember the details. A link on the cancellation page or in the confirmation email works better than a request weeks later.

### How many churn interviews do I need?
Keep going until new interviews stop adding new triggers. For a fairly similar group, one well-known study found saturation within the first 12 interviews. Run more if you compare several segments.

### Will churned customers agree to an interview?
Fewer will book a call than will answer a short interview at a time they choose. Keep it short, explain why you are asking, and don't use it to pitch.
