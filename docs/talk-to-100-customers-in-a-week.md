# How to Talk to 100 Customers in a Week

**In short:** To talk to 100 customers in a week, stop booking a call for each one. Pick one decision, write a short voice interview guide, invite customers you can already reach, and let an AI interviewer run the conversations in parallel while you read transcripts each day. Keep a few live calls for the topics that need a human.

## Why 100 live calls in a week rarely happens

A 30-minute call takes more than 30 minutes. Each one needs an invite, a calendar slot, a reminder, the call, notes and a follow-up. At 100 people, that is several working weeks of calendar time for one person, before anyone reads the notes. Teams that try usually end up with a dozen calls and a backlog of no-shows.

AI-moderated interviews remove the scheduling step. Nielsen Norman Group notes that participants can complete an AI-moderated interview at a time of their choosing, without a human facilitator, much like unmoderated usability testing ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). That is what makes a week-long window realistic: conversations happen whenever customers are free, including at the same time.

## Do you actually need 100?

Ask what the number is for. For one fairly similar group of customers and one clear question, themes often stop changing well before 100. In a widely cited study, thematic saturation occurred within the first 12 interviews, with the basic elements of themes present as early as 6, in a relatively homogeneous group. The authors note that 12 may not be enough when the group is more varied or the question is broad ([Guest, Bunce & Johnson, 2006](https://www.sfu.ca/~palys/GuestEtAl-2006-HowManyInterviewsAreEnough.pdf)).

So 100 makes sense when you have several segments (for example, four plans or five markets with 15 to 25 people each), when you want many concrete examples for a roadmap debate, or when response rates mean you must invite far more people than will finish. It does not turn qualitative interviews into a percentage. If the real question is "how many customers feel this way?", run a survey alongside ([Nielsen Norman Group, Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)).

## A five-day plan

### Day 1: Pick the decision and the segments

Write one sentence: we will talk to [who] to decide [what] by [date]. Then split "who" into segments you will compare, such as new users in their first month, long-time customers, and people who cancelled in the last 60 days. Set a target per segment instead of one big number. If you cannot name the decision, the extra conversations will not help.

### Day 1: Write a short, consistent guide

Aim for 6 to 8 open questions about recent behavior, each with a follow-up you would ask if the first answer is thin. Ask "Tell me about the last time you…" rather than "Would you use…?". Open-ended questions let people answer in their own words and surface things you did not think to ask ([Nielsen Norman Group, Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/open-ended-questions/)). Keep the core questions identical across segments so answers can be compared.

AI interviewers follow the script and probe when an answer is short or unclear, or when you told them to, but they don't chase unexpected insights the way a skilled human does ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). A clear guide matters more here than in a loose live conversation. More help: [how to ask better customer interview questions](https://www.hifrank.ai/blog/how-to-ask-better-customer-interview-questions?utm_source=frank-mcp-docs&utm_medium=docs&utm_campaign=talk-to-100-customers-in-a-week).

### Day 2: Test the interviewer, then invite your own customers

Create the study, add the goal and questions, set a time limit people can finish, and take the interview yourself. Fix anything that sounds robotic or confusing. Then invite people you can already reach: a customer email, an in-app message, a post-purchase or cancellation flow, or a [website widget](https://www.hifrank.ai/docs/website-widget-embedding-frank-on-your-site?utm_source=frank-mcp-docs&utm_medium=docs&utm_campaign=talk-to-100-customers-in-a-week).

Invite more people than you need, because not everyone will finish. If you do not have 100-plus reachable customers in a segment, the bottleneck is recruiting, not interviewing. Frank does not recruit participants; use your network, a community you belong to, or a recruiting service first.

### Days 2 to 4: Let interviews run in parallel, and read daily

Because nobody sits on each call, conversations can run at the same time and at any hour. Use that time to read, not to wait. Each morning, open a handful of full transcripts per segment and note what is new. If a question keeps producing confusion, fix it before the next batch of invites.

### Day 5: Check themes against quotes and write the readout

Group answers into themes, and check every theme you plan to act on against exact quotes in context. Write a one-page readout: what you heard in each segment, how often it came up, what you will do, and what you will not. Stop when new interviews stop adding themes. A method for this step: [how to analyze conversational data](https://www.hifrank.ai/blog/how-to-analyze-conversational-data?utm_source=frank-mcp-docs&utm_medium=docs&utm_campaign=talk-to-100-customers-in-a-week).

## When this plan is a poor fit

Nielsen Norman Group found AI interviewers work best as a complement to human moderation. They are a weak fit when the topic needs deep domain knowledge, when you are still exploring a problem you don't understand, or when people need to show you a workflow on screen ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). In those cases, hold fewer live conversations yourself, and use AI-moderated interviews only for the questions you can already phrase clearly.

## FAQ

### Can I really talk to 100 customers in a week?
Yes, if you can reach that many and you drop the one-call-per-person model. AI-moderated voice interviews let customers answer when they are free, so conversations run in parallel. Your limits become how many people you can invite and how quickly you can read the transcripts.

### Is 100 interviews better than 12?
Only if you need it. In one well-known study, saturation occurred within the first 12 interviews for a relatively homogeneous group. Running 100 is useful across several segments or when you want many examples, but it does not make the findings a statistic.

### Where do I find 100 customers to interview?
Start with people you already reach: recent sign-ups, active users, recent cancellations, buyers. Frank does not recruit participants. If you lack enough customers, use your network or a recruiting service before you run interviews.

### Should I still do live calls?
Yes, for a few. Keep live calls for messy discovery, high-stakes accounts, or anything where someone needs to show you their screen. Use AI-moderated interviews for the consistent set.
