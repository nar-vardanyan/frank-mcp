## Why voice instead of a typed survey

People say more when they speak. In a randomized smartphone-survey experiment with 1,001 respondents, oral answers to open-ended probing questions contained about three times as many words as written ones. The same study found that requests for oral answers produced four to five times more nonresponse, and that both modes surfaced similar themes ([Lenzner, Höhne & Gavras, Journal of Survey Statistics and Methodology, 2024](https://research.uni-hannover.de/en/publications/innovating-web-probing-comparing-written-and-oral-answers-to-open/)).

So voice tends to buy depth, and can cost completion. Two practical lessons follow: make it easy to start (one link, no app, no account), and keep it short. A conversational format also helps, because a follow-up can rescue a thin first answer, which a recorded answer to a fixed question cannot.

## How to choose

1. **Do you need follow-ups?** If you want to know why, use a conversational tool. If you want a spoken answer to a fixed question at survey scale, a recorded-answer tool works.
2. **Who are you asking?** Your own customers: any link-based tool. People you can't reach: a tool with recruiting.
3. **Which languages?** Check the stated count against your markets.
4. **What will it cost for your study?** Multiply minutes per interview by the number of interviews, then add any recruiting.
5. **Is it secure enough?** Look for SOC 2 and GDPR before putting customer voices in it.

## When a voice survey is the wrong tool

If you need to know how many customers feel a certain way, a typed survey with closed questions is still the right instrument ([Nielsen Norman Group, Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)). If the topic is messy or needs someone to show you their screen, run live interviews; Nielsen Norman Group found AI interviewers work best as a complement to human moderation ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).
