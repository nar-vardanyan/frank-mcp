## Start with where the reasons come from

A CRM "closed-lost reason" is a picklist a seller filled in after the deal. Call recordings show what the buyer said to you. Neither is the buyer's own explanation of the decision. The GTM Lead's 2026 guide groups tools the same way: buyer interview platforms (a third party or an AI agent talks to the buyer), call and CRM analytics (patterns across many deals, no extra outreach), and tools that push findings to sellers; it notes buyers "tend to be less candid with someone from the vendor they just declined" ([The GTM Lead](https://thegtmlead.com/blog/win-loss-analysis-software/)). Sybill's roundup sorts tools by evidence type too: conversation-based tools, interview platforms and competitive-intelligence suites ([Sybill](https://www.sybill.ai/blogs/best-win-loss-analysis-software)).

So the first decision is which evidence you need, not which logo to buy.

## The analytics tools

### Gong and Sybill

Gong and Sybill analyze what was said on recorded calls across deals ([Sybill's categories](https://www.sybill.ai/blogs/best-win-loss-analysis-software), [Gong pricing](https://www.gong.io/pricing)). They cover every deal without asking anyone anything, which is their strength. What they cannot show is a reason the buyer never said to you. The GTM Lead describes that as the gap: "what was said to you."

## How many buyers do you need?

Treat a handful as anecdote. The GTM Lead's guide says "a handful of interviews is an anecdote" and suggests asking how many interviews a quarter a plan covers and how the vendor reaches buyers who ignore the request ([The GTM Lead](https://thegtmlead.com/blog/win-loss-analysis-software/)). With a self-serve interviewer, the limit is how many buyers you can invite and how many answer.

## Limits of AI interviews

Nielsen Norman Group found AI interviewers stick to the script and may probe when an answer is short or unclear, work best as a complement to human moderation, and that human interviewers still outperform for high-stakes decisions and messy problem spaces ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). For a large lost deal, keep a human conversation.

## How to choose

1. **Want a vendor offering both human and AI buyer interviews?** Clozd or Klue.
2. **Want patterns across every deal without outreach?** Gong or Sybill.
3. **Want buyers' own words, cheaply, from buyers you can reach?** Frank, then add a vendor for the deals that matter most.
4. **Want both?** Mine calls for patterns, then interview a sample to learn why.

## FAQ

### What is the best win-loss analysis software?
It depends on the evidence you need. For vendor-run buyer interviews, Clozd or Klue Win-Loss. For analysis of recorded calls, Gong or Sybill. For AI voice interviews with buyers you invite yourself, Frank.

### How do I run win-loss interviews?
Interview won and lost buyers soon after the deal closes, ask open questions about the decision, and compare themes by stage. The GTM Lead notes buyers tend to be less candid with someone from the vendor they declined, so use someone other than the account owner or a neutral interviewer.

### Does win-loss software publish prices?
Mostly not. The GTM Lead reports Clozd, Klue, Gong and Crayon list no price; Klue's pricing page shows "Get a demo" and Gong prices per user plus a platform fee.

### Does Frank recruit buyers?
No. Frank does not recruit participants. You invite your own buyers by link or website widget.

### Can AI run win-loss interviews?
Yes, in a limited way. Clozd and Klue both offer AI interviews, and Frank runs AI voice interviews. Nielsen Norman Group says AI interviewers work best as a complement to human moderation, not a replacement.
