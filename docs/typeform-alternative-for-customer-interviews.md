## Why a form isn't an interview

Typeform is a form and survey builder. Its paid plans are priced by monthly responses: Basic at $39/month (100 responses), Plus at $79/month (1,000 responses) and Business at $129/month (10,000 responses), each cheaper when billed yearly ([Typeform, Pricing](https://www.typeform.com/pricing)). That model fits structured questions at volume.

Interviews do a different job. Open questions let people answer in their own words and surface things you didn't think to ask ([Nielsen Norman Group, Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/open-ended-questions/)), and the value comes from the follow-up: "What happened next?" "Why did that matter?" A form asks each question once. An interviewer adapts.

Typeform knows this: it now offers **Research Flow**, which it describes as an end-to-end, AI-powered research workflow that blends structured questions with AI-moderated conversations, with participants from a verified panel or your own audience ([Typeform, Research Flow](https://www.typeform.com/research-flow)). The page offers "Request a demo"; no Research Flow price was shown on the pages reviewed.

## How to choose

1. **Is it a form job or an interview job?** Sign-ups, quizzes and NPS scores: keep a form. Understanding why people churn, stall or buy: use an interviewer.
2. **Who will answer?** Your own customers: Frank, Koji, TheySaid and Voiceform all accept a link. No audience: choose a tool with a panel; Frank does not recruit participants.
3. **Voice or typing?** Voice gets longer answers but can cost completion. In a randomized smartphone experiment, oral answers had about three times as many words as written ones, with four to five times more nonresponse ([Lenzner, Höhne & Gavras, 2024](https://research.uni-hannover.de/en/publications/innovating-web-probing-comparing-written-and-oral-answers-to-open/)). Keep voice studies short.
4. **Budget model?** Typeform prices by responses; interview tools price by minutes, credits or interviews. Estimate the cost of the study you'll actually run.

## When this is a poor fit

If you need to know how many customers feel a certain way, a form or survey is the right instrument ([Nielsen Norman Group, Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)). And Nielsen Norman Group found AI interviewers work best as a complement to human moderation, not for messy discovery or deep domain topics ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).
