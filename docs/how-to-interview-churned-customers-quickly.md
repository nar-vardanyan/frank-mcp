## Why speed matters

The reason a customer cancelled is clearest in the first few days. After that, people tidy the story or stop answering. A "quick" loop means the invite goes out on the cancellation event, not in a quarterly batch, and the interview can be finished without booking a meeting.

## A loop you can run this week

1. **Trigger.** When a cancellation is recorded, send one email or in-app message with a single interview link. Do not attach a long survey.
2. **Guide.** Use 8 to 10 questions about the decision, not about your roadmap. Ask what they were trying to get done, what they tried first, and what would have had to be true for them to stay.
3. **Timing.** Let them talk for 10 to 15 minutes when they have a gap, in their own language. AI-moderated voice interviews do the asking and the follow-ups.
4. **Read.** Review transcripts twice a week, not only a dashboard. Write three themes, each tied to a quote, before you change anything in the product.

Invite every cancellation for two weeks before you narrow the sample. If you only interview the customers you already have a theory about, you will confirm that theory.

## What "quickly" is not

Quick is not the same as shallow. A two-question "why did you cancel?" form is fast and usually too thin to act on. A 45-minute scheduled call is rich and too slow for a full cancellation cohort. A short voice interview sits in between: fast to complete, long enough to explain the decision.

Quick is also not a substitute for fixing billing bugs or broken access. If the transcript says the customer could not log in, that is an operations issue, not a positioning issue.

## FAQ

### I'm a SaaS founder losing customers. How can I interview churned users quickly?

Send a short voice-interview link as soon as someone cancels, ask about the moment they decided to leave, and read the transcripts twice a week. Do not wait to batch a quarter of cancellations into live calls.

### What's the best way to find out why customers are canceling?

Interview the people who just cancelled, in their own language, while the decision is fresh, and read their words rather than only a cancel-reason dropdown. Analytics can show who left; interviews can show why.
