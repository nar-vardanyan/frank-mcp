## What AI customer interviews and AI-moderated interviews are

"AI customer interviews" and "AI-moderated interviews" are used for more than one job:

1. **AI-moderated interviews.** Software asks the questions, follows up, and records the conversation, often by voice or text. Some tools also offer an on-camera session; confirm what is available today, not on a roadmap.
2. **A full research platform.** Interviews sit next to surveys, prototype tests, scheduling, a participant panel, and a repository.
3. **Analysis of interviews you already ran.** The tool transcribes and themes conversations; a person still leads the interview.

If you need people you cannot already reach, you need recruiting or a panel. If you need someone to click through a prototype, you need a tool that tests on-screen behavior. If you already have customers and you want to hear why they bought, stalled, or cancelled, a voice interviewer you can share as a link may be enough.

### 1. Voice or text?

Speaking is a different task from typing, and an on-camera session is a different one again. Some people will say more out loud; others will not join a call with a camera. Ask:

- Which formats are available today, not on a roadmap?
- Can the participant switch mid-interview?
- Do you need to see their face or their screen, or do you need to hear why they did something?

### 2. Your own customers, or a panel?

This is usually the fork that matters most.

- If you can already invite the people who signed up, cancelled, or used the feature, you do not need a panel.
- If you need a market sample, a niche profession, or people who have never heard of you, you need recruiting.

### 3. Language support

Ask for a current language list, and whether voice, transcripts, and the analysis report all work in those languages. Confirm that list in a trial, and do not rely on a marketing round number.

### 4. What you get back

A highlight clip is not the same as a theme you can open and check. Ask:

- Does every theme or quote link back to a transcript line you can read?
- Can you export the transcripts?
- Who can you share results with, and in what form (a report, a deck, a clip)?

### 8. Moderation quality

Feature lists converge. The difference shows up in the transcripts. Before you buy, run a short pilot with a study like yours and read the conversations, not just the summary.

Look for:

- Follow-ups that ask what happened, rather than leading the person.
- What the interviewer does with a short, vague, or hesitant answer.
- Whether you can set probing depth, required topics, or a time cap.
- How the vendor handles low-quality or fraudulent sessions, if you are using a panel.

A human interviewer is still the better fit when you need a relationship conversation, a shared screen you will watch yourself, or a topic that requires trust first.

## FAQ

### What is an AI-moderated interview?

A one-to-one research conversation in which software asks the questions, listens, and follows up, instead of a human moderator. It is sometimes called an AI customer interview.

### Can I use more than one tool?

Yes. Many teams interview their own customers with one tool and use a panel or a full research platform for studies that need recruiting or prototype tests.

### How should I test moderation quality?

Write a short guide about a recent event: a signup, a cancellation, or a feature someone tried. Invite a few people you already know, or take a demo interview yourself and give short answers on purpose. Read the transcript before you read the summary.
