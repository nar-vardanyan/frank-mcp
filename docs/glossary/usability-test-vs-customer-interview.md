# Usability test vs customer interview

A usability test watches someone try a specific task in a product or design so you can see where they get stuck. A customer interview asks about a real event or job in their life, often with no design in front of them. The first studies whether people can use something; the second studies whether it matters and why.

Updated November 27, 2026.

## How it's used

Use a usability test when you have a flow, a screen, or a prototype and you need to see whether people can complete a task and where they hesitate. Use a customer interview when you need the story around a job, a purchase, a cancel, or a missed feature, including what they did before they met your product.

The two methods can follow each other. An interview can tell you which job to design for. A usability test can tell you whether the design supports that job. An interview after launch can tell you why a used or unused feature did not change the person's workflow.

## Example

Suppose you shipped a new export feature and few people use it.

- In a usability test, you might watch a current user try to export a report, and note where the labels, steps, or errors get in the way.
- In a customer interview, you might ask someone who saw the feature and skipped it: Walk me through the last time you needed to get this data out. What did you do instead? That interview can show that the job happens in another tool, or that they never needed the export at all.

Both can be useful. They are not substitutes for each other. For unused features, start with [why users don't adopt a new feature](../why-users-dont-adopt-a-new-feature.html). For task-centered interview questions, see [customer interview questions for UX researchers](../customer-interview-questions-for-ux-researchers.html).

## Common mistakes

- Treating a smooth usability session as evidence that people want the product. Completing a task is not the same as hiring the product in real life.
- Using an interview to ask people to imagine clicking through a design they cannot see. If you need task behavior, run a usability test.
- Putting a prototype in front of someone and only asking Do you like this? That is neither a careful usability test nor a strong interview.
- Skipping interviews after a usability test, then being surprised that a usable flow still is not used.
