# Leading question

A leading question is a question that nudges the person toward a particular answer, often by smuggling in a preference, a cause, or a compliment. It makes agreement easy and makes disconfirming evidence harder to hear. Open questions about a recent event are usually a clearer starting point.

Updated November 27, 2026.

## How it's used

Interviewers watch for leading questions when they write a guide, and again when they follow up live. The risk is highest when you already have a hypothesis, such as they left because of price, or when you want the person to like your idea.

A leading question can be a tag that suggests the answer (That was frustrating, right?), a why that already names the cause (Why was the pricing too high?), or a compliment trap (What did you love about the new design?).

## Example questions

Leading:

- You cancelled because it was too expensive, right?
- How much do you love the new dashboard?
- Wouldn't this save your team a lot of time?
- Why is onboarding confusing?

Clearer, about a recent event:

1. Walk me through what happened before you cancelled. What was going on?
2. What did you notice first when you opened the dashboard that day?
3. The last time you tried to get this job done, what did you do, and how long did it take?
4. Where did you get stuck during setup, and what did you try next?

The first set tells the person what you hope to hear. The second set leaves room for a different story.

## Common mistakes

- Stacking two questions, one of them leading, so the person answers the easier prompt.
- Following a good open question with a leading probe that steers the next sentence.
- Using a survey scale, then reading the result as the reason. Convert the item to an open question about a real event if you need the why. See the [survey to interview question converter](../tools/survey-to-interview-converter/).
- Assuming an AI interviewer will correct leading wording for you. It will usually ask what you wrote.
