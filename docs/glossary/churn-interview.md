# Churn interview

> **TL;DR.** A churn interview is a conversation with a customer who cancelled, downgraded, or stopped using a product, held to learn what triggered that decision and what they did instead. It focuses on a recent event, not a general opinion of the product. The aim is to hear the story around the cancel or downgrade, including who else was involved. A SaaS founder can run these with people already on a billing or cancel list, soon after they leave, without waiting for a formal research project.

If you are a SaaS founder losing customers, the question is usually not whether to interview people who cancelled. It is how to hear from them quickly, while the decision is still easy to recall, without building a research team or begging people to book a call. You already have the contacts: a cancel flow, a billing list, a downgrade event, or a last-session record. A cancel-page survey can collect a reason at the moment of leaving. That is useful, and it is not a churn interview. A checkbox that says "too expensive" often stands in for a workflow that broke, a champion who left, or a job the product no longer did well enough. The interview is how you hear that story: what happened in the days before the cancel, who else was involved, what they had already tried, and what they use now.

Speed matters because cancelled customers rarely owe you a calendar slot, and because memory fades. A live call is still the right tool when the account was large, the relationship is messy, or you need to hear from more than one person on a committee. ## Playbook

### 1. When to reach out

Reach out soon after the cancel, downgrade, or last session, while the decision is still easy to recall. Waiting months forces people to reconstruct a choice they barely remember, or to give you a smoothed story that leaves out the trigger. A founder does not have to wait for a quarterly churn review. A short message after each cancel is enough to keep the queue fresh.

Say clearly that you want to learn what happened, not to win the account back in the conversation itself. If the person thinks this is a retention call, they will decline, or they will give you a polite version of the survey reason. You can still offer a path back to customer success after the interview, but the interview itself is for the story.

There are times to wait a few days: a tense billing dispute, a person who is clearly mid-migration, or a cancellation that is still being reversed. There are also times to skip a live booking and send a link instead, because a cancelled customer will often talk for ten minutes on their own schedule and will not accept a meeting invite. In every case, the clock that matters is their memory of the decision, not your reporting calendar.

If the only signal you have is a silent non-renewal, treat the renewal date, or the last meaningful session, as the event, and ask about that period.

### 2. Who to invite

Invite people from your own cancel flow, billing list, downgrade events, or product records. You already know who left. Frank AI Researcher does not recruit participants. A founder who cannot name the last ten cancelled accounts does not have an interview problem yet. They have a records problem.

Interview more than the person who clicked cancel when a buying committee, a manager, or a finance lead made the call. The person who submitted the cancellation is often the one who was asked to do it, not the one who decided. If you can see other contacts on the account, invite a user and a decision-maker, and keep the guide consistent so you can compare what each person cared about.

Aim for a mix of reasons and account types, not only the customers who already wrote you a thoughtful goodbye. People who chose "other," who gave no reason, or who downgraded instead of cancelling often hold the missing story. People who moved to a workaround, or switched to another product, can tell you what job they still have and what they compared.

You do not need a fixed interview count to start. Take the recent cancellations you can already reach, keep the guide stable, and look at whether new conversations are still changing the picture. See [saturation](saturation.html). If the same trigger and the same workaround keep showing up, you have something you can take to the product and success teams. If every interview is a new story, tighten the segment (plan, role, or time-to-value) rather than collecting a larger pile of one-off quotes.

### 3. What to ask

Ask about a recent event, not a general review of the product. A short, consistent [interview guide](interview-guide.html) helps you compare answers: the trigger, the workarounds they tried, who decided, and what they use now. Keep the questions open and non-leading. "Why did you hate the product?" leads, and it skips the trigger and the alternative. So does "Was it the price?" The person can say yes to both and still leave out the week when they stopped getting value.

A short guide for a SaaS founder can stay close to this pattern:

1. Walk me through what happened in the days before you cancelled. What triggered the decision?
2. Who else was involved, and what did each person care about?
3. What had you already tried in the product before you decided to leave?
4. What do you use instead now, including a workaround or doing the job by hand?
5. Was there a moment when staying would have been possible? What would have had to be true?

Useful follow-ups stay with the event: "What happened next?", "How did you decide?", "What did you try before that?", and "If you had to explain this to a colleague who missed it, what would you say?" If they mention price, ask what they were comparing it with, and what would have made the current price feel fair. If they mention a missing feature, ask what they did instead, and when they first needed it.

Leave out anything that turns the conversation into a save-the-account pitch, a request for a testimonial, or a feature-by-feature rating. If you already have written cancel reasons, the [cancellation reason sorter](../tools/cancellation-reason-sorter/) can group those answers into themes and suggest follow-up interview questions.

### 4. What to do with the answers

The value is in the comparison, not in any single pointed quote. Read the interviews together. For each person, capture the trigger, who else was involved, what they had already tried, what they use now, and whether staying would have been possible. Then look across the set for repeated stories.

A useful readout for a founder and a small team is short: the main triggers, the workarounds people moved to, who actually decided, what you will change in the product or the success motion, and what you will not do on the basis of a single interview. Attach one exact quote to each theme, and open the transcript before a quote goes into a roadmap or a board update. A summary can turn "we cancelled because onboarding never reached the person who had to use it" into "they said it was too expensive."

Use the cancel-page survey, or a reason code in billing, as a starting point, not as the finding. When a survey says "price" and the interviews say "we could not get a second team onto it," believe the story and treat the checkbox as a label. When interviews and surveys agree, you can be more confident. Share the readout with product and success. A churn interview that stays in a notebook does not change the product.

If someone wants to come back, hand that to success after you have finished learning. The habit that makes this work at a small company is a short, regular pass from cancel to invite to readout, not a one-time project after a bad month.

## Approaches compared

A SaaS founder can learn why customers leave in several ways. They are not interchangeable. A live call is still the right tool when the account is sensitive or the story is political. An async exit survey is a fit when you need a reason at the moment of leaving, and counts across many cancels. An AI-moderated voice interview is a fit when you want the story and cannot book every call. Billing notes and reason codes are useful context, but they are usually written from the company's point of view. A win-loss interview on a lost deal is a different job, even when the customer later cancelled something else.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting months, then asking people to reconstruct a decision they barely remember.
- Interviewing only the person who clicked cancel when a buying committee or a manager made the call.
- Asking Why did you hate the product? That leads, and it skips the trigger and the alternative.
- Treating a single survey checkbox, such as too expensive, as the full explanation. Price is often the easy label for a value or workflow problem.
- Turning the interview into a save-the-account call. You will hear a negotiation, not the story.
- Publishing a theme without opening the transcript. A summary can turn an onboarding failure into "they did not like the product."
- Stopping after two angry interviews and treating that as the whole churn picture. Look at quiet cancels too, and at whether new conversations are still changing what you think. See [saturation](saturation.html).

## FAQ

### How can I interview churned users quickly?

Invite people from a recent cancel, downgrade, or last-session list you already have, and reach out while the decision is still easy to recall. Use a short, consistent guide, and give them a way to talk without booking a call when they will not take a meeting. Frank AI Researcher is a voice-first product that runs AI-moderated voice interviews with cancelled customers you invite. It does not recruit participants, and it does not replace a live call when the account is relationship-heavy.

### What is the best way to find out why customers are canceling?

There is no single best way for every product. An exit survey or cancel-page form is a fast way to collect a reason at the moment of leaving, and to see how often a label appears. A churn interview is how you hear the story behind that reason: the trigger, who else was involved, what they had already tried, and what they use now. Many founders use a survey to find a pattern, then interview a smaller set of people about that pattern.

### Should I use an exit survey instead of a churn interview?

Use a survey when you need counts, or a reason logged in the cancel flow. Use a churn interview when you need the story. A survey is a poor substitute for an interview, and an interview is a slow way to get a percentage. The two work well together.

### Can a SaaS founder run churn interviews without a research team?

Yes, if you can already reach people who cancelled, downgraded, or stopped using the product. Write a short non-leading guide, invite those people, and compare themes across interviews. A research team or a skilled human interviewer helps when the account is sensitive, a committee is involved, or you need facilitation on a messy relationship. You do not need a research team to start.

### Does Frank recruit cancelled customers for churn interviews?

No. Frank AI Researcher does not recruit participants. You invite people from deals or accounts you already have, for example from a cancel flow, a billing list, or product records.

### How soon after a cancel should I run a churn interview?

Run it soon after the cancel, downgrade, or last session, while the decision is still easy to recall. Waiting months makes it harder for people to remember the trigger and the tradeoffs, and the people who decided may have moved on.
