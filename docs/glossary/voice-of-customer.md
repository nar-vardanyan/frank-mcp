# Voice of customer

Voice of customer (VoC) is the practice of collecting and organizing what customers say about their needs, the words they use, and their experience, then using that evidence in product and messaging decisions. Interviews, support tickets, reviews, and surveys can all feed a VoC program. Interviews help you hear the reasons behind those words.

Updated November 27, 2026.

## How it's used

A VoC program is a way to keep customer language and evidence in one place, then use it when you write messaging, prioritize a roadmap, or investigate a drop in use. Surveys and tickets tell you how often something comes up. Interviews explain why it matters and how people describe it in their own words.

Customer, success, marketing, and product teams often share the same VoC notes so they are not each running disconnected interviews.

## Example questions

Use questions that pull out the customer's words and a recent event:

1. How would you describe what you hired this product to do, in your own words?
2. Walk me through the last time you recommended it, or chose not to. What did you say?
3. When the product missed something, what sentence did you use to explain the gap to a colleague?
4. What almost made you look for something else?

For campaign or onboarding work, see [customer interview questions for marketers](../customer-interview-questions-for-marketers.html) and [customer interview questions for customer success teams](../customer-interview-questions-for-customer-success-teams.html).

## Common mistakes

- Collecting quotes and never connecting them to a decision. A VoC library is only useful if someone uses it when they choose copy, a feature, or a fix.
- Treating a word cloud or a single score as the voice of the customer. Counts help, but they do not replace the story.
- Interviewing only your happiest customers, then writing messaging that sounds like a testimonial reel.
- Mixing internal jargon into the questions, then being surprised when customers do not use those words.
