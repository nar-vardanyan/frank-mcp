# The Mom Test

The Mom Test is a set of rules for customer interviews from Rob Fitzpatrick's book The Mom Test. You ask about the person's past behavior in a real situation, not whether they like your idea. Compliments, hypothetical answers, and promises about the future are weak evidence, because even a supportive person can mislead you when the question is poor.

The book is [The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com) by Rob Fitzpatrick. This page is a short definition, not an excerpt, and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the author.

Updated November 27, 2026.

## How it's used

Teams use The Mom Test as a check on interview questions, especially in discovery. The point is to collect evidence from real past events: what someone did, what they already pay for, and where they got stuck.

It is useful when founders or product people talk to people they know, because a kind audience will often try to be encouraging. A good question still works if the person wants you to succeed. A weak question fails in that setting.

## Example questions

Stronger questions, about a recent event:

1. Walk me through the last time this problem came up. What did you do?
2. What have you already tried, and what happened?
3. When did you last spend time or money on this? What did you pick?
4. Who else got involved, and what did they care about?

Weaker questions, which invite compliments or guesses:

- Do you think this is a good idea?
- Would you use this?
- Would you pay for this?
- How much would this help you?

## Common mistakes

- Hearing I would definitely use that and treating it as demand. It is a compliment or a guess, not a past action.
- Asking about a hypothetical future, then building as if the answer were a commitment.
- Pitching the product during the interview and then asking for an opinion of the pitch.
- Copying a list of questions without checking whether each one asks about a real event.
