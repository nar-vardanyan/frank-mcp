## Why recent deals, not a yearly batch

Buyers forget the real decision quickly. A conversation two weeks after the yes or no will usually beat a review six months later. Aim to invite every closed-won, closed-lost, and walked-away opportunity in a set window, such as the last 14 or 30 days, rather than sampling only the deals you already have a story about.

## Who to invite

Invite the person who owned the decision, not only the champion who took the demo. On a small B2B team that often means 8 to 15 people a month, not hundreds. You already have their email from the deal. If you cannot name the buyer, fix that in your CRM before you buy more software.

Interview both wins and losses. Wins tell you what to repeat. Losses tell you what to stop guessing about. Abandoned deals often reveal a stall you can fix in the sales process.

## What to ask

Keep the guide short, 8 to 10 questions, and let the interviewer follow up. Useful starters:

1. What were you trying to get done when you started looking?
2. What other options did you consider, including doing nothing?
3. What almost stopped the decision?
4. Who else was involved, and what did they care about?
5. If you could change one thing about the process, what would it be?

Invite the buyer from each recent deal, share a short voice interview they can complete in their own time, and read the transcripts for wins and losses together. Do this inside two to four weeks of the decision.
