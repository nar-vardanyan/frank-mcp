## What are AI-moderated interviews?

An AI-moderated interview is a one-to-one research conversation in which software asks the questions, listens, and follows up, instead of a human moderator. You still set the goal and write or approve the guide. The customer joins, usually from a link, and answers in their own words. The software returns a transcript, and often a summary or themes you can check against the original conversation.

The same idea is sometimes called an AI customer interview or an AI interview. The important part is who leads the conversation: an AI moderator, not a person on a scheduled call.

Frank AI Researcher runs this method by voice. Customers speak; they do not fill in a form, and they do not join a video or chat interview.

## How a customer takes one

1. You write a short guide about a recent event: a signup, a cancellation, a purchase, or a feature someone tried.
2. You share an interviewer link, or embed a website widget.
3. The customer opens the link when they have time and talks to the AI moderator.
4. The moderator asks your questions and follows up when an answer is thin.
5. You read the transcript and check any theme you will act on against exact quotes.

Yes, customers actually talk to the AI moderator. In a Frank interview, that conversation is spoken. For more on [whether people talk to an AI interviewer](https://www.hifrank.ai/blog/do-people-talk-to-ai?utm_source=frank-mcp-docs&utm_medium=docs&utm_campaign=ai-moderated-interviews), see Frank's write-up.

Nielsen Norman Group describes AI interviewers as a voice that asks questions, and notes that participants can complete the interview at a time of their choosing, without a human facilitator ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).

## When to use them

AI-moderated interviews are a fit when you already know what to ask, and you can already reach the people you want to hear from. Nielsen Norman Group says the method works well for collecting structured input at scale in that situation, and that the AI sticks to your script and may probe when an answer is short or unclear, but does not chase unexpected insights the way a skilled human does ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).

Practical cases:

- **Discovery with a clear job.** You want to hear how people already do a task, not whether they like an idea. See [How to validate a startup idea with customer interviews](validate-a-startup-idea-with-customer-interviews.html).
- **Churn and cancellation.** You can invite people who just cancelled, and you want the story behind a short reason code.
- **Feature or onboarding feedback.** You already shipped something, and you want to know where people got stuck.
- **Win-loss.** You can invite buyers who chose you or did not, soon after the decision.

They complement surveys: interviews explain why, and a survey can later measure how many people share that reason ([Nielsen Norman Group, Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)). A longer comparison is in [AI customer interviews vs surveys](ai-customer-interviews-vs-surveys.html).

## When a human moderator is better

A human moderator is still the better fit when:

- The conversation depends on a relationship, trust, or a key account.
- You do not yet understand the problem, and each talk may need to go somewhere new.
- Someone needs to show you their screen, or you need to watch them use a product.
- The topic needs deep domain knowledge that a scripted moderator will miss.

Nielsen Norman Group found AI interviewers work best as a complement to human moderation, not a replacement, and lists messy discovery, deep domain topics, and on-screen tasks as poor fits ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). They also note that AI interviewers cannot read facial expressions, so they cannot always tell whether someone is still thinking.

## What you still do yourself

An AI moderator does not replace your judgment.

- You pick the decision the interviews are meant to inform.
- You write or approve the questions.
- You invite your own customers. Frank does not recruit participants.
- You check summaries and themes against exact quotes before you act.
