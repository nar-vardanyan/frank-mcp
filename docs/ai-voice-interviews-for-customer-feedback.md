**In short:** AI voice interviews are short spoken conversations in which an AI interviewer asks your customers open questions, follows up when answers are thin, and turns each conversation into a transcript and themes. Customers join from a link when they have time, so you get the "why" behind feedback without scheduling calls. They complement, not replace, live interviews and surveys.

## What an AI voice interview is

An AI voice interview replaces the human moderator on a customer call with an AI interviewer that speaks. You write the goal and questions. The customer opens a link, talks, and the AI asks follow-ups. Nielsen Norman Group describes AI interviewers as a voice that asks questions, and notes that participants can complete the interview at a time of their choosing, without a human facilitator ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).

It is different from:

- **A survey with a voice-recording field.** That collects one answer per question with no follow-up.
- **A phone survey (IVR).** That reads fixed questions and captures keypad or short spoken responses.
- **A live interview.** That has a human who can read the room and change course at any moment.

## Why voice for customer feedback

Speaking is easier than typing for most people, so open questions get longer, more specific answers. People tell stories out loud that they would never type into a text box: what they were trying to do, what went wrong, what they did next. Those stories are what turn feedback into a decision.

The trade-off is control. Nielsen Norman Group found AI interviewers stick to the script and may probe when an answer is short or unclear, or when told to, but don't chase unexpected insights the way a skilled human does. They also can't read facial expressions, so they can't always tell whether someone is still thinking ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).

## Good use cases

Nielsen Norman Group says AI-moderated interviews are best when you already know what to ask, such as product feedback, and lists multilingual interviews without translators as another fit ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). In practice, for customer feedback that means:

- **Post-launch feature feedback.** What people expected, what they did, where they got stuck.
- **Churn and cancellation.** The story behind "too expensive" or "not using it" ([churn interviews](https://www.hifrank.ai/use-case/ai-churn-interviews)).
- **Onboarding.** Why new users stall in their first week.
- **Win-loss.** Why a buyer chose you or someone else ([win-loss](https://www.hifrank.ai/use-case/win-loss-analysis)).
- **Feedback across markets.** The same questions in several languages.

## How AI voice interviews compare

| | Survey | AI voice interview | Live interview |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answers | Mostly closed-ended ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/)) | Spoken, open answers with follow-ups when answers are short or unclear ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)) | Open, with any follow-up |
| Scheduling | None | None; people choose when ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)) | A slot per person |
| Answers "how many?" | Yes | No | No |
| Answers "why?" | Partly | Yes | Yes, most deeply |
| Best for | Measuring across many people | Consistent "why" conversations with many customers | Messy discovery and key accounts |

Interviews and surveys work well together: use interviews to find the reasons, and a survey to size them ([Nielsen Norman Group, Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)).

## How to run one, step by step

### 1. Start from a decision
Write one sentence: we will hear from [who] to decide [what]. Feedback without a decision becomes a pile of quotes.

### 2. Write 6 to 8 open questions
Ask about recent, specific events ("Tell me about the last time you…"), each with a follow-up for thin answers. Open questions let people answer in their own words ([Nielsen Norman Group, Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/open-ended-questions/)). More help: [how to ask better customer interview questions](https://www.hifrank.ai/blog/how-to-ask-better-customer-interview-questions).

### 3. Test it yourself
Take the interview before anyone else. Listen for questions that sound robotic, run long, or confuse.

### 4. Invite customers where they already are
Put the [interviewer link](https://www.hifrank.ai/docs/navigating-your-ai-interviewer-dashboard) in an email, an in-app message or a cancellation flow, or embed a [website widget](https://www.hifrank.ai/docs/website-widget-embedding-frank-on-your-site). Frank does not recruit participants; you invite your own customers.

### 5. Read transcripts and check themes against quotes
Group answers into themes, and check each theme you'll act on against exact quotes in context. Stop when new interviews stop adding themes; for a fairly similar group that is often within about a dozen conversations ([Guest, Bunce & Johnson, 2006](https://www.sfu.ca/~palys/GuestEtAl-2006-HowManyInterviewsAreEnough.pdf)). A method for this step: [how to analyze conversational data](https://www.hifrank.ai/blog/how-to-analyze-conversational-data).

## What to look for in an AI voice interview tool

- **Follow-up quality.** Does it probe thin answers without leading?
- **Transcripts you can check.** Themes should link back to exact quotes.
- **Languages.** Can customers answer in their own language?
- **Length and minutes.** Does the plan allow interviews long enough for your guide?
- **Security.** Look for SOC 2 and GDPR compliance before you put customer conversations in it.
- **Participants.** Most tools, Frank included, expect you to bring your own customers.
- **Agent access.** Can you set up studies and read results from your AI assistant?

## When AI voice interviews are a poor fit

Nielsen Norman Group lists cases to avoid an AI interviewer: topics that need deep domain knowledge, early exploration of a problem you don't understand, studies where each conversation should take a different path, and anything where people need to show you their screen ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). For those, run live interviews yourself.
