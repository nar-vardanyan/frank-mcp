## When to use this guide

Use these question groups when you are still proving the problem, tightening the first version of a product, or learning why someone started or almost did not start. Talk about a recent event in their work or life, not a pitch of your idea. Invite people you already know, such as a waitlist, early users, or past customers. Frank does not recruit participants. Replace [product] and [problem] with plain words they use.

## Questions: a recent problem

Use this group in discovery, before or just after you have a first version, when you need to hear how the problem shows up in a real week.

1. Walk me through the last time [problem] happened. What started it?
   Follow up if the first answer is thin: How often has that happened lately, and what did it cost you in time or money?
2. What did you do to deal with it that day, including any tool, workaround, or person you asked for help?
   Follow up: What worked, and what still felt unfinished?
3. What have you already tried, and why did you stop or stay with it?
   Follow up: If you could no longer use that approach tomorrow, what would you do instead?

## Questions: early use and almost not starting

Use this group with people who tried your product, or who said they would and then delayed, when you want the real start moment.

1. Walk me through the moment you first decided to try [product]. What was going on that week?
   Follow up: What almost made you wait?
2. After you started, when did you first get a useful result, and what was still confusing?
   Follow up: What would you have needed earlier?
3. Tell me about a moment you almost stopped using [product]. What triggered that?
   Follow up: What made you continue, or what would have brought you back?

## Questions: willingness to pay and current spend

Use this group when people already pay for something in this category, or when you need to hear how a purchase would be decided, without asking them to set your price for you.

1. What do you pay today, if anything, to deal with [problem], and what does that get you?
   Follow up: Who approves that spend, and what would make them question it?
2. Think about the last time you paid for a new tool in this area. What convinced you it was worth it?
   Follow up: What almost blocked the purchase?
3. If [product] went away tomorrow, how would that affect your week, and what would you replace it with?
   Follow up: What would you need to see before you would pay to keep that result?

## FAQ

### What should a founder ask in a customer interview?

Ask about a recent problem, what they already tried, the moment they started or almost did not start, and what they already pay to deal with the problem. Use the question groups on this page as a starting guide, and stay with events that already happened rather than a pitch of your idea.

### Does Frank recruit early customers or a waitlist for me?

No. Frank AI Researcher does not recruit participants. You invite people you already know, such as a waitlist, early users, or past customers.

### How is this different from a survey about my idea?

These questions ask someone to walk through a recent event and what they already do. That is usually more useful than rating a hypothetical product. You can still run a short survey for counts, then use this guide when you need the why.
