# How to Get User Feedback Without Scheduling Calls

**In short:** You can get interview-depth user feedback without a calendar by sharing an AI-moderated voice interviewer as a link or a website widget, so people talk when they have time. Surveys and in-app forms also skip scheduling, but they rarely follow up; use those when you need a comparable number, and use interviews when you need the why.

## Why scheduling gets in the way

A live interview is still the right choice when you need a relationship conversation or a shared screen. The cost of that method is the calendar: you have to find a time, show up, and moderate each call. That is why many teams default to a survey or skip talking to users altogether.

You do not have to make that trade. Nielsen Norman Group describes an AI-moderated interview as a live conversation facilitated by an AI system with a synthetic voice. Like unmoderated usability testing, participants can complete the interview at a time of their choosing, without a human facilitator, which is convenient for both sides ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).

## When a survey is enough

Choose a survey or an in-app form when the decision depends on a number you can compare, and you already know the likely answers. "What share of trial users would be disappointed if the product went away?" is a survey question. Nielsen Norman Group places surveys as a quantitative, attitudinal method, which is a good fit for those "how many" questions and a poor fit for "why" questions ([Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)).

A short form in a cancellation flow or after a task can also be a first pass: it tells you which category people pick. It usually cannot tell you which competitor, which feature, or which moment was behind that category.

## When you still need an interview, but not a meeting

Choose an interview when you need the story behind a number, or when you do not yet know which answers to put on a survey.

Good questions for an unscheduled interview look like:

- Walk me through the moment you decided to cancel. What almost made you stay?
- Tell me about the first time you tried to get value from the product. Where did you get stuck?
- Why did you choose this option, the other option, or nothing? What almost tipped it the other way?

In your interview tool, create the study, add the goal and questions, and test the interviewer yourself. Then share an [interviewer link](https://www.hifrank.ai/docs/navigating-your-ai-interviewer-dashboard) by email, in-app message, or a cancellation flow, or embed a [website widget](https://www.hifrank.ai/docs/website-widget-embedding-frank-on-your-site) so people can start without leaving your site. They talk with the AI interviewer by voice when they have time. You read the transcripts and themes afterwards.

Invite your own customers: people who recently signed up, cancelled, bought, or used the feature. Frank does not recruit participants. If you have no customers to invite yet, a recruiting service or your network is a better first step.

With Frank, interviews can run in parallel, because nobody has to moderate each one live. The stopping rule is still saturation, not a large round number. In Guest, Bunce and Johnson's study, saturation occurred within the first 12 interviews for a relatively homogeneous group, with the basic elements of themes present as early as 6 ([Guest, Bunce & Johnson, 2006](https://www.sfu.ca/~palys/GuestEtAl-2006-HowManyInterviewsAreEnough.pdf)).

## When you should still schedule a call

Nielsen Norman Group found that AI-moderated interviews work best as a complement to human moderation, not a replacement. Avoid relying on an AI interviewer when the topic needs deep domain knowledge, when you are still exploring a problem you do not understand, when each conversation should take a different path, or when people need to show you a workflow on a screen rather than only tell you about it ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).

In those cases, book a live conversation. You can still use an unscheduled AI interview to cover the consistent questions, then follow up live with the people whose answers need a deeper look.

## FAQ

### How can I get user feedback without scheduling calls?
Share an AI-moderated voice interviewer as a link or website widget, so people talk when they have time, or send a survey if you need a comparable number. Interviews are better for why; surveys are better for how many. Neither method requires a calendar.

### Is a survey the same as an unscheduled interview?
No. A survey is usually a closed-ended way to measure attitudes across a larger group. An AI-moderated voice interview is a qualitative conversation: the interviewer asks open questions, can follow up, and you read the transcripts and themes. Nielsen Norman Group places surveys as a quantitative, attitudinal method, and AI-moderated interviews as a way to collect structured qualitative input.

### Will people actually complete an interview if I do not book a time?
They can, because they join when it suits them. Nielsen Norman Group notes that this scheduling flexibility is convenient for both participants and researchers. You still have to invite people you can already reach, keep the interview short enough to finish, and test it yourself before you share it.

### When should I still book a live call?
Book a live call when the conversation depends on a relationship, a shared screen, or a messy problem you do not yet know how to probe. Nielsen Norman Group says AI-moderated interviews are a poor fit in those situations and should supplement human-led interviews, not replace them.

### Does the AI find participants for me?
No. For most product decisions, invite your own customers: people who recently signed up, cancelled, bought, or used the feature. Frank does not recruit participants. If you have no customers yet, a recruiting service or your network is a better first step.

### How many unscheduled interviews should I run?
Keep going until new interviews stop adding themes. In one widely cited study, saturation occurred within the first 12 interviews for a relatively homogeneous group, with the basic elements of themes present as early as 6. Broader questions or mixed audiences often need more.
