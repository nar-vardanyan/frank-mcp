## What "at scale" should mean

Scale, in this context, means many consistent "why" conversations, not a survey-sized percentage. Nielsen Norman Group describes AI-moderated interviews as a way to collect structured input at scale, especially when you already know what to ask, such as post-launch product feedback. It also says they should supplement human-led interviews, not replace them, and that they are not yet a fit for messy, in-depth discovery work ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).

The stopping rule is still saturation. In one well-known study, thematic saturation occurred within the first 12 interviews, with the basic elements of overarching themes present as early as 6, in a relatively homogeneous group. Twelve is often not enough if the group is relatively heterogeneous, the data quality is poor, or the question is broad ([Guest, Bunce & Johnson, 2006](https://www.sfu.ca/~palys/GuestEtAl-2006-HowManyInterviewsAreEnough.pdf)). Running more interviews than that can still be useful when you have several segments, or when you want a wider set of examples, but the extra volume does not turn the findings into a tally.

If the decision is really "how many people feel this way?", a survey is the better instrument ([Nielsen Norman Group, Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)). Interviews and surveys also work well together ([Nielsen Norman Group, Mixed-Methods Research](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mixed-methods-research/)).

## When this method is a poor fit

Nielsen Norman Group's study of two AI interviewers found they work best as a complement to human moderation. Avoid relying on an AI interviewer when the topic needs deep domain knowledge, when you are still exploring a problem you do not understand, when each conversation should take a different path, or when people need to show you a workflow on a screen rather than only tell you about it ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).

Scale does not remove those limits. If the decision depends on a relationship conversation or on watching someone work, interview those people yourself, even if you use an AI interviewer for the broader set.

## Step by step

### 1. Pick a decision that many similar conversations can inform

Write one sentence: we will interview [who] to decide [what]. Good scale questions have a consistent shape, such as why people cancelled last month, or where new users get stuck in onboarding. If you cannot say how a finding would change a decision, tighten the question first.

### 2. Write a short, consistent interview guide

Aim for about 6 to 8 open questions about recent behavior, each with a follow-up you would ask if the first answer is thin. Ask "Tell me about the last time you…" rather than "Would you use…?". Keep a core set of questions the same so answers can be compared. Nielsen Norman Group notes that AI interviewers stick to the script and probe when an answer is short or unclear, or when you told them to, so a clear guide matters more than it does in a loose human interview ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).

### 3. Invite customers you already have

The AI interviewer does not find people for you. Invite people who recently signed up, cancelled, bought, or used the feature you are studying. Frank does not recruit participants. If you have no customers to invite yet, use your network or a recruiting service first. That is a separate step from running the interviews.

### 4. Share a link or widget, and let interviews run in parallel

In your interview tool, create the study, add the goal and questions, and test the interviewer yourself before you share it. Then share an [interviewer link](https://www.hifrank.ai/docs/navigating-your-ai-interviewer-dashboard?utm_source=frank-mcp-docs&utm_medium=docs&utm_campaign=how-to-interview-customers-at-scale) by email, in-app message, or a cancellation flow, or embed a [website widget](https://www.hifrank.ai/docs/website-widget-embedding-frank-on-your-site?utm_source=frank-mcp-docs&utm_medium=docs&utm_campaign=how-to-interview-customers-at-scale) so people can start without leaving your site.

Participants join when they have time and talk with the AI interviewer by voice. Because nobody has to be on a call with each person, interviews can run at the same time.

### 5. Read the transcripts and themes, then stop at saturation

As interviews complete, open the transcripts and the themes. Check that each theme you will act on is backed by an exact quote, and open a few full transcripts to confirm the quote is in context. Write a short readout: what you heard, how often, what you will do, and what you will not. Stop when new interviews stop adding themes, or tighten the question and run another short round. A larger count is not a finish line.

## FAQ

### How do I interview customers at scale?
Use an AI-moderated voice interviewer for consistent conversations people can complete when they have time, instead of scheduling each call yourself. Invite customers you already have, let interviews run in parallel, and read the transcripts and themes. Keep going until new conversations stop adding themes.

### Does interviewing at scale mean I can skip talking to customers myself?
No. Nielsen Norman Group found that AI-moderated interviews are a complement to human moderation, not a replacement, and that they are a poor fit for messy discovery, deep domain knowledge, or studies where people need to show a workflow on a screen. Use an AI interviewer for the consistent set, and interview some people yourself when the conversation depends on a relationship or a shared screen.

### How many interviews are enough if I can run a lot of them?
The stopping rule does not change because you can run more. In Guest, Bunce and Johnson's study, saturation occurred within the first 12 interviews for a relatively homogeneous group, with the basic elements of themes present as early as 6. Broader questions or mixed audiences often need more. Extra interviews can add coverage; they do not turn the study into a percentage.

### Do I need a research panel to interview customers at scale?
Only if you do not already have people to invite. Frank does not recruit participants. If you can already reach the customers whose decision you are studying, share a link or website widget with them. If you have no customers yet, a recruiting service or your network is a better first step.

### Can interviews at scale replace a survey?
No. Interviews answer why; surveys answer how many. Nielsen Norman Group places surveys as a quantitative, attitudinal method, and AI-moderated interviews as a way to collect structured qualitative input. Use both when you need the reason and the scale.
