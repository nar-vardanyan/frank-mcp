## What "interviews customers for you" can cover

A customer interview study has five jobs. An AI interviewer always does the third; the others vary by tool.

1. **Design:** turn a goal into an interview guide.
2. **Recruit:** find and invite the right people.
3. **Moderate:** hold the conversation, ask follow-ups.
4. **Analyze:** transcripts, themes, quotes.
5. **Report:** turn findings into something you can share.

Nielsen Norman Group describes AI-moderated interviews as a way to collect structured input at scale when you already know what to ask; the AI sticks to your script and may probe when an answer is short or unclear, but doesn't chase unexpected insights ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). So even with an AI interviewer, the decision and the questions are still yours.

## What an AI interviewer still can't do for you

- **Pick the decision.** It can draft questions, but only you know what you'll change.
- **Read the room.** Nielsen Norman Group notes AI interviewers can't read facial expressions or tell whether someone is still thinking ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)).
- **Replace all live interviews.** The same study found AI interviewers work best as a complement to human moderation, and are a poor fit for messy discovery or deep domain topics.
- **Count.** Interviews tell you why; surveys tell you how many ([Nielsen Norman Group, Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)).
- **Check its own summaries.** Always confirm the themes you act on against exact quotes.

## A simple first study

1. Write one sentence: we will interview [who] to decide [what].
2. Draft 6 to 8 open questions about recent behavior, with a follow-up for each ([Nielsen Norman Group, Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/open-ended-questions/)).
3. Take the interview yourself and fix what sounds off.
4. Invite people who just did the thing you're studying: signed up, cancelled, bought.
5. Read transcripts as they arrive; stop when new ones add no new themes. For a fairly similar group, that is often within about a dozen ([Guest, Bunce & Johnson, 2006](https://www.sfu.ca/~palys/GuestEtAl-2006-HowManyInterviewsAreEnough.pdf)).
