## How to use these prompts

Use these prompts within a few weeks of a decision, while the evaluation is still easy to recall. Buyers are often more candid with someone who is not their sales rep, and a voice interview from a link asks less of them than another meeting.

Paste a prompt into ChatGPT, fill in the [brackets], and edit the output before you use it. The prompts follow the order of a study: plan it, write the guide, write the invite, analyze the transcripts, then decide. Remove names, emails, and anything sensitive you do not need before you paste survey answers or transcripts.

## Prompts to plan the study

### 1. Define what the win-loss program should inform

```text
I sell [product] to [buyer type]. In the last [period] we won some deals, lost some, and some ended in no decision. Help me define the one decision this win-loss study should inform, and list the questions our CRM loss reasons cannot answer.
```

### 2. Choose which deals to invite

```text
Here are recent deals with stage, size, segment, and recorded reason, without contact details: [paste]. Suggest which deals to invite first for wins, losses, and no-decisions, and explain the reasoning. Prefer recent decisions.
```

### 3. Decide who in the buying group to interview

```text
For a [deal type] deal, which roles in the buying group should we interview: the champion, the economic buyer, a technical evaluator, someone else? For each role, write what they are likely to know that the others do not.
```

## Prompts to write the discussion guide

### 4. Draft win and loss guides

```text
Write two 10-minute voice interview guides for [product], one for deals we won and one for deals we lost. Cover what started the search, how they handled the problem before, which options they considered (including doing nothing or building it themselves), who joined the evaluation, when one option became more or less credible, where price first came up and who raised it, and the deciding factor. Stay neutral about every vendor. No yes/no questions. One follow-up each.
```

### 5. Draft a no-decision guide

```text
Write a third version for deals that ended in no decision: what made proceeding difficult, what the team is doing instead now, and what would restart the evaluation. Do not assume another vendor won.
```

### 6. Write the study introduction

```text
Write a short introduction for the start of the interview: it is a voice conversation with an AI interviewer, it takes about 10 minutes, it is not a sales follow-up, nothing they say will reopen the deal, and they can skip any question.
```

## Prompts to write the invite

### 7. Email to a buyer from a lost deal

```text
Write an email to a buyer from a deal we lost [number] weeks ago, inviting them to a voice interview of about 10 minutes they can take from a link. Thank them for the time they spent evaluating, say the goal is to learn, not to reopen the deal, and keep it under 90 words. Signed by [name, role], who is not their sales rep. Leave [link] as a placeholder.
```

### 8. Email to a buyer from a won deal

```text
Write a version for a buyer from a deal we won, asking to hear what nearly stopped the purchase and what they had to explain internally to get approval. Under 70 words.
```

## Prompts to analyze transcripts

### 9. Extract the decision story

```text
Below are win-loss interview transcripts, each labeled won, lost, or no decision: [paste]. For each one, extract the trigger for the search, the options considered, the people involved, the moment confidence changed, how price came up, and the deciding factor in the buyer's words. Keep what the buyer said separate from any conclusion you draw. One exact quote per field. Do not invent quotes.
```

### 10. Compare buyer reasons with CRM reasons

```text
Here is the reason recorded in our CRM for each of these deals: [paste]. Compare it with the deciding factor from the interview. List where they match, where they differ, and what the differences suggest about how we record reasons.
```

## Prompts to synthesize themes

### 11. Group the deciding factors

```text
Group the deciding factors across wins, losses, and no-decisions. For each factor, show which outcomes it appears in, two quotes, and whether sales, product, pricing, or marketing could change it. Mark single-interview findings as low confidence.
```

### 12. Write the win-loss readout

```text
Write a win-loss readout for sales and product leads: why we win, why we lose, no-decision patterns, three changes to test next quarter, and the quotes behind each. List what buyers told us that we did not already believe separately from what confirmed our view.
```

## FAQ

### What should I ask in a win-loss interview?

Ask what started the search, which options they considered (including doing nothing), who was involved, when one option became more or less credible, where price came up, and what decided it. Use a separate version for no-decision deals, and stay neutral about every vendor.

### Can ChatGPT run win-loss interviews by itself?

No. On its own, ChatGPT can help you choose deals, write guides and invites, and analyze transcripts you paste in. To have the interviews run by voice with buyers, connect a tool that conducts them. Frank AI Researcher is available as an app in ChatGPT and runs AI-moderated voice interviews with buyers you invite.

### Does Frank contact buyers for me?

No. Frank does not recruit participants or contact buyers. You send the interviewer link to buyers from your own deals.

### Who should send the win-loss invite?

Someone other than the buyer's sales rep, such as a product lead or founder. Say plainly that the goal is to learn and that the interview will not reopen the deal.
