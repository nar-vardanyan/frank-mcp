## Collect, analyze, or both?

Qualitative research has two halves. First you gather open-ended data: interviews, recordings, open survey answers. Then you make sense of it: transcripts, codes, themes, quotes, reports.

AI now helps with both, but in different products. AI interviewers moderate conversations: they stick to your script and may probe when an answer is short or unclear, but don't chase unexpected insights the way a skilled human does ([Nielsen Norman Group, AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). Analysis platforms transcribe, tag and summarize data from many sources. Knowing which half you need avoids paying for the wrong one.

## How to choose

1. **No data yet?** Start with a collection platform. If you have customers to invite, a link-based interviewer like Frank is the quickest start. If not, choose one with recruiting.
2. **Lots of data already?** Start with an analysis platform to make sense of existing interviews, calls and tickets.
3. **Both?** Pair them. Collect with an interviewer, then export transcripts or connect via MCP to your analysis tool or assistant.
4. **Check the evidence trail.** Whatever you pick, every theme should link back to an exact quote, and you should read a few full transcripts before acting.

## Limits of AI in qualitative research

Nielsen Norman Group found AI interviewers work best as a complement to human moderation, and are weaker for messy discovery, deep domain topics or anything someone must show on screen ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). AI summaries can also over-generalize; check them against the source. And qualitative counts are not percentages; for "how many," use a survey ([Nielsen Norman Group, Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)).
