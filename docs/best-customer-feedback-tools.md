## Match the tool to the question

"Customer feedback" covers four jobs that need different tools:

| Job | Question it answers | Tool type |
|---|---|---|
| Measure | How many customers feel this? Is it improving? | Survey |
| Collect requests | What do customers want built? | Feedback board |
| Aggregate | What are thousands of comments about? | Voice-of-customer analysis |
| Understand | Why did they do it? | Interviews |

Nielsen Norman Group draws the line between quantitative questions (how many or how much) and qualitative ones (why something occurs or how to fix it) ([Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)). Surveys, boards and analytics sit on the first side. Interviews sit on the second.

A popular roundup, Comms Advisor's guide (last updated 7 October 2026), names Zonka Feedback for NPS and CSAT workflows, Typeform for high-completion surveys, Canny for product feedback boards, Unwrap for AI feedback analysis and Medallia or Qualtrics for enterprise VoC ([Comms Advisor](https://commsadvisor.com/best-customer-feedback-software/)). The word "interview" does not appear anywhere in that page's HTML (checked 19 November), which is the gap this page fills.

## How to choose

1. **Do you need a number or a reason?** A number points to a survey tool. A reason points to interviews.
2. **How much feedback do you already have?** If you have thousands of comments, tickets and reviews, start with analysis. If you have very little, start by collecting it.
3. **Who answers?** Surveys and boards reach anyone who visits. Frank interviews customers you invite; it does not recruit, so you need a way to reach them, such as email, an in-app message or your website.
4. **Who reads it?** Pick tools whose output your team will actually read every week. A theme is only useful if it links back to what a customer said.

## A simple feedback stack

1. **One survey** for the metric you track over time.
2. **One board** if customers request features.
3. **One interview link** for the moments where you need the reason: cancellation, trial end, a low score.
4. **A weekly read** of ten transcripts alongside the dashboard.

Interview the low scorers: a survey tells you who is unhappy, and an interview tells you why. More on the split: [AI customer interviews vs surveys](https://nar-vardanyan.github.io/frank-mcp/ai-customer-interviews-vs-surveys.html).

## Limits of AI interviews

Nielsen Norman Group found AI interviewers stick to the script, may probe when an answer is short or unclear, and are best as a complement to human moderation rather than a replacement ([AI-Moderated Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). They are a way to hear more customers, not a substitute for talking to your most important ones.
