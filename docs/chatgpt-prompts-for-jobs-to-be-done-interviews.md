## How to use these prompts

Use these prompts when you want to understand why people hire your product, or fire it, in a particular situation. JTBD interviews work well when they reconstruct one recent switch in order, rather than asking for opinions about features.

Paste a prompt into ChatGPT, fill in the [brackets], and edit the output before you use it. The prompts follow the order of a study: plan it, write the guide, write the invite, analyze the transcripts, then decide. Remove names, emails, and anything sensitive you do not need before you paste survey answers or transcripts.

## Prompts to plan the study

### 1. Define the switch to study

```text
I run [product] for [customer type]. Help me define the switch to study: what people switched from (including a spreadsheet or doing nothing), what they switched to, and how recent the switch should be for them to remember it well. Ask me up to five questions first.
```

### 2. Write hypotheses as forces

```text
Write hypotheses for this study as the four forces of a switch: what pushes people away from their old way, what pulls them toward [product], what makes them anxious about switching, and what habits keep them where they are. Mark each as a guess to test.
```

### 3. Choose who to interview

```text
Describe who to interview: customers who switched to [product] recently, and, if I can reach them, people who switched away. Explain what each group can tell us. I will invite people from my own lists.
```

## Prompts to write the discussion guide

### 4. Timeline guide for a recent switch

```text
Write a 10-minute voice interview guide that reconstructs one recent switch to [product] as a timeline: the first thought that something had to change, what they did to look for options, what they compared, the moment they decided, and the first use after. Ask for specific moments, places, and people. One follow-up per question.
```

### 5. Probes for each force

```text
Add follow-up probes for each force: what was frustrating about the old way, what made the new option attractive, what worried them about switching, and what almost kept them with the old way.
```

### 6. Version for people who switched away

```text
Write a version of the guide for customers who left [product] for something else, using the same timeline from first doubt to switching away.
```

## Prompts to write the invite

### 7. Email to recent switchers

```text
Write a short email to customers who started using [product] recently, inviting them to a voice interview of about 10 minutes about how they decided to make the change, which they can take from a link when it suits them. Say we want to hear the story of the decision, not feedback on features. Under 90 words. Leave [link] as a placeholder.
```

### 8. Message to people who switched away

```text
Write a short, respectful message to former customers asking for the same kind of voice interview about their decision to move on. Make clear it is not a save attempt.
```

## Prompts to analyze transcripts

### 9. Build each switch timeline

```text
Below are JTBD interview transcripts: [paste]. For each one, build a timeline of the switch: first thought, passive looking, active looking, deciding, and first use, with an exact quote for each stage. Write "not mentioned" when a stage is missing. Do not invent quotes.
```

### 10. Code the four forces

```text
For each transcript, list the pushes, pulls, anxieties, and habits the person described, each with an exact quote. Then show which forces appear across several interviews and which appear only once.
```

## Prompts to synthesize themes

### 11. Write job statements

```text
From the timelines and forces, write job statements in the form "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]", using the customers' own words where possible. For each statement, list the interviews and quotes that support it.
```

### 12. Write the JTBD readout

```text
Write a one-page readout: the jobs we found, the situations that trigger them, the forces that help and block switching, what this means for positioning, onboarding, and the roadmap, and open questions. Report interview counts as counts, not percentages.
```

## FAQ

### What is a jobs-to-be-done interview?

A JTBD interview reconstructs one recent switch, from the first thought that something had to change to the first use of the new option, to understand the situation and the progress the person wanted. The approach is associated with Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta.

### What are the four forces in a JTBD interview?

Pushes away from the old way, pulls toward the new option, anxieties about switching, and habits that keep people where they are. Coding interviews by these forces shows what helps and blocks a switch.

### Can ChatGPT run JTBD interviews by itself?

No. On its own, ChatGPT can help you plan the study, write guides and invites, and code transcripts you paste in. To have the interviews run by voice, connect a tool that conducts them.
