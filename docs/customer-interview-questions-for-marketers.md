## When to use this guide

Use these question groups when you need to change a homepage, an ad, a launch email, or category language, and you want the words customers already use. Invite people from a recent campaign, a trial list, or your customer list. Frank does not recruit participants. Replace [product] and [campaign] with the names they would recognize.

## Questions: why they chose you

Use this group with people who recently became a customer or started a trial, when you want the real path and the message that landed.

1. Walk me through how you first heard about [product]. What were you looking for at the time?
   Follow up if the first answer is thin: Where were you, and what had just happened that made you look?
2. What did you think [product] was for, based on the first page, email, or post you saw?
   Follow up: Which line or claim made you stay, and which almost made you leave?
3. What else did you consider, including doing nothing, and what tipped the choice?
   Follow up: What would have made you pick something else?

## Questions: campaign or page mismatch

Use this group when a campaign, landing page, or launch drove visits or trials that did not convert, or that converted and then stalled.

1. Tell me about the [campaign] or page that brought you in. What did you expect to happen next?
   Follow up: Where did the next step differ from that expectation?
2. What felt unclear, overpromised, or aimed at someone else?
   Follow up: What would you have needed to see to continue with confidence?
3. After you arrived, what almost made you leave before you tried [product] for real?
   Follow up: Was it the offer, the proof, the wording, or the steps you had to take?

## Questions: the words they use

Use this group when you are rewriting positioning, or when sales and marketing use different language from customers.

1. Before you found [product], how did you describe [problem] to a colleague, or search for a way to fix it?
   Follow up: Which words would you never use for this?
2. Which words on our site or in our emails sound like you, and which sound like us talking to ourselves?
   Follow up: What would you write on the first screen if you had to explain [product] to a peer?
3. When would you recommend [product], and how would you describe it in one sentence?
   Follow up: What would you warn them about, or tell them to ignore?

## FAQ

### What should a marketer ask in a customer interview?

Ask how they first heard about the product, what they thought it was for, what else they considered, where a campaign or page did not match the next step, and how they already describe the problem. Use the question groups on this page as a starting guide.

### Does Frank recruit an audience for campaign interviews?

No. Frank AI Researcher does not recruit participants. You invite people from a recent campaign, a trial list, or your customer list.

### When should I run this instead of looking at campaign metrics?

Use metrics to see where people drop. Use this guide when you need the words and expectations behind that drop, especially after a launch, a new landing page, or a trial that stalled. The interviews do not replace the counts.
