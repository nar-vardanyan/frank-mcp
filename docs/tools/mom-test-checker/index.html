About these rules

The checks are simple heuristics, inspired by Rob Fitzpatrick's book The Mom Test, which explains how to talk to customers without getting misled by polite answers. The rules on this page are our own wording and pattern matching. They are not taken from the book, and the tool is not affiliated with or endorsed by the author. Read the book for the full method.

Pattern matching can be wrong in both directions. A flagged question can be fine in context, and a clean result does not prove a question is good. Use the result as a second look, then edit by hand.

How to ask better interview questions

  • Ask about their life, not your idea. People are kind about ideas. They are accurate about what they did last week.
  • Ask about the past, not the future. "Tell me about the last time..." gets a real story. "Would you use..." gets a guess.
  • Ask what they spend today. Money and time already spent on a problem show how much it matters. A price for something imaginary does not.
  • Keep questions open. A yes/no question ends the story before it starts.
  • Don't pitch during the interview. Once you describe your product, people start reviewing it instead of telling you about their problem.
