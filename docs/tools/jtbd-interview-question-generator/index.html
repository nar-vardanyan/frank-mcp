JTBD switch interview: why people switched to Acme Invoicing

Who to interview: Freelance designers who started using Acme Invoicing in the last 90 days and took part in the decision, while it is still fresh.

Length: about 15 to 20 minutes, 8 questions.

Opening: Thanks for making time. I'd like to hear the story of how you came to use Acme Invoicing, from the very first moment you thought about changing. There are no right answers, and I'm not here to sell you anything. It takes about 15 to 20 minutes.

First thought

Find the first moment the old way stopped being good enough.

  1. PushThink back to before you used Acme Invoicing. When did you first feel you needed something better than spreadsheets? What happened that day?Probe: Keep going back in time until they reach the very first moment. Ask what else was going on at work or at home.
  2. HabitWhat did you still like about spreadsheets at that point?Probe: This is the habit force. Ask how long they had done it that way and what made it good enough.

The event and active looking

The moment interest turned into a search, and what they compared.

  1. PushWhat happened that made you start looking seriously? Why then and not a month earlier?Probe: Look for a dated event: a deadline, a cost, a complaint, a new boss, a failure. Ask how much time passed since the first thought.
  2. PullWhich options did you look at, including doing nothing or building something yourself? Where did you find them?Probe: Ask how each option got onto the list and which one they almost picked.

Deciding

The pull of the chosen option and the worries at the moment of choice.

  1. PullWhat made you pick Acme Invoicing over the others? Tell me about the moment you knew.Probe: If they name a feature, ask what that feature let them do that they couldn't do before.
  2. AnxietyWhat worried you just before you signed up or paid? What made the worry go away?Probe: Ask what reduced the worry: a free plan, a review, a colleague, a refund policy.

First use and the new normal

Compare the progress they expected with what they got.

  1. ProgressTell me about the first time you used Acme Invoicing. What did you do first, and what happened?Probe: Ask what they expected it to do that it did, or did not do. A gap here is a churn risk.
  2. HabitWhat do you still miss about spreadsheets?Probe: Habits that survive the switch show where people may drift back.

What is a JTBD switch interview?

A switch interview is a Jobs to Be Done method for learning why people change from one way of doing something to another. Instead of asking what people want, you ask them to tell the story of one real switch, from the first moment the old way felt wrong to the first weeks with the new one. The story shows the job they were trying to get done, in their own words. You can run it with people who switched to your product, or with people who switched away from it.

The four forces of progress

Two forces push people toward a change, and two hold them back. Every question in the guide is tagged with the force its answer usually reveals.

ForceWhat it isExample question from the guide
PushPulls toward change: the problems with the current way that make people start looking.Think back to before you used [your product]. When did you first feel you needed something better than [the old way]? What happened that day?
PullPulls toward change: the outcome people picture with the new option.After that first thought, what did you notice about other ways to do this, before you started looking properly?
AnxietyHolds people back: worries about the new option, such as cost, effort or risk.What kept you from changing at that point?
HabitHolds people back: comfort with the current way, even when it is not great.What did you still like about [the old way] at that point?

The timeline, phase by phase

  1. The switch. Anchor the story on the day they started. For example: "Roughly when did you start using [your product]? Where were you and what were you doing when you signed up?"
  2. First thought. Find the first moment the old way stopped being good enough. For example: "Think back to before you used [your product]. When did you first feel you needed something better than [the old way]? What happened that day?"
  3. Passive looking. What they noticed before they searched in earnest. For example: "After that first thought, what did you notice about other ways to do this, before you started looking properly?"
  4. The event and active looking. The moment interest turned into a search, and what they compared. For example: "What happened that made you start looking seriously? Why then and not a month earlier?"
  5. Deciding. The pull of the chosen option and the worries at the moment of choice. For example: "What made you pick [your product] over the others? Tell me about the moment you knew."
  6. First use and the new normal. Compare the progress they expected with what they got. For example: "Tell me about the first time you used [your product]. What did you do first, and what happened?"

Tips for running switch interviews

  • Talk to recent switchers. People remember the details of a decision best while it is fresh, so aim for the last few months.
  • Talk to the person who decided. A user who had a tool chosen for them can tell you about use, not about the switch.
  • Ask for the story, not the reasons. "What happened next?" gets better answers than "Why did you choose it?"
  • Don't name the forces. Nobody can answer "what was your anxiety?" Ask about the moment, and listen for the force.
  • Keep going until the stories repeat. Stop when new interviews stop adding new events, worries or alternatives.

About this method

The switch interview and the four forces of progress (push, pull, anxiety and habit) come from the Jobs to Be Done work of Bob Moesta, Chris Spiek and The Re-Wired Group. Their own walkthrough of the forces of progress diagram is a good place to start. The questions on this page are our own wording, and this tool is not affiliated with or endorsed by them.

FAQ

What is the difference between a switch interview and a normal customer interview?

A normal interview often asks about needs and opinions. A switch interview follows one real decision in order, from the first thought to the first weeks after, so the answers are facts about what happened rather than guesses about what people want.

Can I use it with customers who cancelled?

Yes. Choose "Customers who left you" and the guide asks about the story of leaving: the first sign it stopped working, the event that made them go, what they chose instead and what they still miss.
