Churn interview guide: B2B SaaS

Welcome (say this): Thanks for talking with me. This is a short conversation about a recent time you decided to cancel the software. It is not a sales call, and there are no right or wrong answers.

  1. Think back to when you first signed up for the software. What was going on that made you look for something like it?Follow-up if the answer is short: What had you tried before?
  2. Walk me through the moment you decided to cancel the software.Follow-up if the answer is short: What happened right before that?
  3. What had changed, compared with when you started?Follow-up if the answer is short: When did you first notice it?
  4. Who else on your team was involved in the decision to cancel, and what did they care about?Follow-up if the answer is short: Who had the final say?
  5. What almost made you stay?Follow-up if the answer is short: What happened after that?
  6. What do you use now, including a spreadsheet or "nothing"?Follow-up if the answer is short: How did you pick that?
  7. What did the software do well for you, even if it wasn't enough?Follow-up if the answer is short: Is there anything you miss now?
  8. What, if anything, would have changed your mind, and when would it have needed to happen?Follow-up if the answer is short: Did you tell anyone about it at the time?

Close: Thank you. That's everything I wanted to ask.

Tips

  • Talk to people within a few weeks of leaving, while the real reason is still fresh.
  • Separate the trigger (the moment they started looking) from the reason (why they were open to leaving).
  • Don't defend the product or explain features. If they missed something, write it down and move on.

Which interview goal should I pick?

Churn
Why customers stopped using or cancelled. Talk to people who left recently.
Win-loss
How buyers chose between you, a competitor, or doing nothing. Talk to recent buyers from deals you won and lost.
Discovery
How people get a job done today and where it breaks down, before you decide what to build. Replace [the job] with the customer's words.
Pricing
How customers judge value and cost, based on what they actually paid and decided. Talk to people who bought, didn't buy, or downgraded.

How the questions are written

  • About a recent, real event. "Tell me about the last time…" gets a story. "Would you use…?" gets a guess.
  • Open and neutral. No question hides the answer you hope to hear, and none asks people to price a hypothetical plan.
  • One follow-up each. Use it only when the first answer is short. Stay in the story: "What happened next?"
  • 8 questions. Enough for about 10 to 15 minutes with follow-ups. Cut any question that does not help the decision you are making.
