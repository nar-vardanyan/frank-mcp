Questions that are already open and about a real event are kept as they are. Background questions (role, company size) become one warm-up question. Near-duplicates are merged, and long pastes are capped at 20 questions, because an interview works best with 6 to 10.

Why interview questions differ from survey questions

  • A survey measures how many; an interview finds out why. A score or a tick box tells you the size of something. A story tells you what caused it.
  • Ask about the past, not the future. "Tell me about the last time…" gets a real story. "Would you use…?" gets a guess, often a polite yes.
  • One thing at a time. A question about two things gets half an answer.
  • Don't hide the answer in the question. "Don't you think it's easier?" asks people to agree with you.
  • Never ask people to price a hypothetical plan. Ask what they pay today and how they decided.

FAQ

Does this use AI?

No. It uses fixed rules that spot common survey patterns and rewrite them. The same input always gives the same output. Read the result and edit anything that sounds off before you use it.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

Yes, it is free and needs no login. It runs in your browser, and nothing you paste is sent to a server or stored.

Should I stop running surveys?

No. Surveys are good at measuring how many people feel a certain way. Interviews explain why. Many teams run a short survey, then interview a handful of the people who answered.

Does Frank find people to interview?

No. Frank does not recruit participants. You invite customers you already have, for example the people who answered your survey.

Can I set up the interview from an AI assistant?

Yes. Frank has a remote MCP server at https://api.hifrank.ai/mcp, included on every plan, including Free. Works with ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and Muse, plus other MCP clients that support remote servers with OAuth.
