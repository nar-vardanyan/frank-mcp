What the research says

Every number on this page comes from one of these studies. Each link goes to the published paper.

  • Guest, G., Bunce, A. & Johnson, L. (2006). How Many Interviews Are Enough? An Experiment with Data Saturation and Variability. Field Methods, 18(1), 59 to 82. In 60 in-depth interviews, saturation occurred within the first twelve interviews, and basic elements for metathemes were present as early as six.
  • Hennink, M. & Kaiser, B. N. (2022). Sample sizes for saturation in qualitative research: A systematic review of empirical tests. Social Science & Medicine, 292, 114523. Across 17 empirical studies, saturation was reached within 9 to 17 interviews, particularly with relatively homogenous populations and narrowly defined objectives. Outliers such as multi-country research needed larger samples.
  • Griffin, A. & Hauser, J. R. (1993). The Voice of the Customer. Marketing Science, 12(1), 1 to 27. Interviews with 20 to 30 customers should identify 90% or more of the customer needs in a relatively homogeneous customer segment.
  • Malterud, K., Siersma, V. D. & Guassora, A. D. (2016). Sample Size in Qualitative Interview Studies: Guided by Information Power. Qualitative Health Research, 26(13), 1753 to 1760. Information power: the more information the sample holds that is relevant for the study, the fewer participants are needed. It depends on the aim of the study, sample specificity, use of established theory, quality of dialogue and analysis strategy.

The studies measure "saturation": the point where new interviews add little or nothing new. Most of them looked at fairly similar groups of people with a narrow question, so treat the ranges as a starting point, not a rule.

  • Segments multiply the range. The studies count interviews within one fairly similar group, so each segment gets its own range.
  • What you already know moves you within the range. A specific question and lots of prior knowledge point to the lower end; a new topic points to the upper end. This follows the idea of information power from Malterud and colleagues.
  • Very mixed people push you up. Split them into segments if you can.
  • Check early. Review your notes after about 6 interviews per segment, when basic themes often appear (Guest, Bunce and Johnson).

FAQ

How many customer interviews are enough?

There is no single number. Published studies found that interviews with fairly similar people and a narrow question stop turning up new themes within about 9 to 17 interviews, and that finding most customer needs in a segment takes about 20 to 30. Use the range for your goal, per segment, and stop when new interviews stop adding new themes.

Why per segment?

The studies count interviews within one fairly similar group of people. If you talk to two very different groups, such as small teams and large companies, each group needs its own set of interviews.

Is this a statistical sample size calculator?

No. Interviews are qualitative, so there is no margin of error to calculate. The planner gives guidance ranges from published saturation studies and explains which one applies.

Does this use AI? Is anything sent to a server?

No. It applies fixed rules in your browser and sends nothing anywhere.
