# How to Measure Product-Market Fit with Customer Interviews

**In short:** A product-market fit survey can tell you whether people would miss your product. It usually cannot tell you why. ## What the score can and cannot tell you

The best-known product-market fit survey, described by Sean Ellis, asks recent users: "How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?" The usual answers are very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, or not disappointed. Ellis treated 40% "very disappointed" as a benchmark for fit ([Sean Ellis, Free Customer Development Help – Survey.io](https://www.startup-marketing.com/free-customer-development-help-surveyio/)).

The percentage is a count. It does not explain the main benefit, what people would use instead, or what holds "somewhat disappointed" users back. A short open-text box on a survey rarely gets a follow-up. That is the job of an interview.

A survey or interview count is also only as good as the people you asked. This test is meant for people who have already used the product, not for people who have only heard of it. Ellis recommended sending the survey to people who have experienced the core of the product, used it at least twice, and used it in the last two weeks ([same source](https://www.startup-marketing.com/free-customer-development-help-surveyio/)). Invite that kind of recent user, not people who have only heard of the product.

## A five-step way to measure it

1. **Decide what you will do with the result.** For example: whether to keep investing in the current benefit, or what to fix before you spend more on acquisition.
2. **Ask the Sean Ellis question.** Use the standard wording and the three answers, so you can count them. You can do this in a survey, or as the first question of a voice interview.
3. **Interview for the why.** For each answer, ask what made them choose it, what the main benefit is, what they would use instead, and what they expected that is missing. The guide below is a starting list.
4. **Segment the answers.** Compare what "very disappointed" and "somewhat disappointed" users say. The first group often tells you what to protect; the second often tells you what is in the way.
5. **Size and decide.** Use the percentage, if you have enough recent users to treat it as a score, together with the interview themes. With a small group, treat both as qualitative input. Write down what you will change, what you will not, and who owns it.

Do not treat interview counts as the PMF percentage. Count the closed Sean Ellis answers separately, from the survey or from the transcripts.

## Sample interview guide

Copy and edit this list. Replace [product] with your own words. Ask about a recent time they used the product, not a hypothetical future.

1. How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]? Very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, or not disappointed?
2. What made you choose that answer?
3. What is the main benefit you get from [product]?
4. Tell me about the last time [product] really helped you. What were you doing?
5. What type of person do you think would benefit most from [product]?
6. What would you use instead if [product] were no longer available?
7. What did you use before [product]? What made you switch?
8. What stops you from using [product] more often?
9. What did you expect [product] to do that it does not?
10. If we could change only one thing, what should it be?

Open questions let people answer in their own words ([Nielsen Norman Group, Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/open-ended-questions/)). Ask about a specific recent episode rather than a general opinion ([The Critical Incident Technique in UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/critical-incident-technique/)).

## FAQ

### What is the Sean Ellis test?

A one-question product-market fit survey: "How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?", answered very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, or not disappointed. Sean Ellis proposed 40% "very disappointed" as a benchmark for fit ([Free Customer Development Help – Survey.io](https://www.startup-marketing.com/free-customer-development-help-surveyio/)).

### Who should I interview?

People who have already experienced the core of the product, used it at least twice, and used it in the last two weeks, which is the group Ellis recommended for this survey. Do not use this test on people who have only seen a landing page.

### Does Frank calculate the product-market fit percentage?

No. Frank records the conversation in a transcript and groups themes across interviews. You count how many people gave each Sean Ellis answer, from the transcripts or from a separate survey.

### Why interview instead of only surveying?

The score says whether people would miss the product. Follow-up questions explain why, what they would use instead, and what holds others back. Surveys are typically more closed-ended than interviews ([Nielsen Norman Group, Should You Run a Survey?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/should-you-run-a-survey/)).

### Can I run this from my AI assistant?

Yes. Connect Frank via MCP and ask the assistant to draft the study and to split results by the Sean Ellis answer. You still approve publishing and invite your own users.
